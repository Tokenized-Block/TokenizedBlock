Map of a human brain: its parts, its connections, its emotions

The real shape of an average human brain (fsaverage5 surface), its 82 regions at their real positions, and the connections measured between them in 207 adults (Human Connectome Project). Visit a part of the brain, or pick an emotion: what studies reported lights up, and impulses travel along the measured connections. It shows what was measured in people; it feels nothing.
. Drag to turn · wheel or two fingers to zoom · tap a glowing dot to walk its connections. Coloured dots: the real brain surface, one colour per part (each surface point takes the part of the nearest region centre: our approximation). Curves: measured connections of what is lit, in the colour of the part they reach (brighter = stronger; drawn curved by us). Moving lights: impulses, by our rule. Dashed gold: known pathways with no measured strength.

What is measured, what is ours

  • Measured: the brain's shape and where each region lies (real surface), the strength of the connections between regions (diffusion MRI tractography, 207 adults), which regions each study reported for an emotion.
  • Ours: the grouping into parts and their one-line "known for" (plain textbook summaries), the curves, the rule by which impulses travel (more often along stronger connections), their speed, the colours, and the mapping of each study's areas onto these 82 regions (stated per emotion).
  • A region-level map, not neurons: about 86 billion neurons and no complete human neuron-level connectome exist. The finest human map here has 82 regions; the fly's page shows a real neuron-level circuit.
  • No region belongs to one emotion: studies report shared regions, and the page counts them.
