A human brain, wired — region by region

The real shape of an average human brain (fsaverage5 surface), its 82 regions at their real positions, and the connections measured between them in 207 adults (Human Connectome Project). Pick an emotion: the regions studies reported for it light up, and impulses travel along the measured connections. It shows what was measured in people; it feels nothing.
. Drag to turn · wheel or two fingers to zoom · shift-drag to move · tap a region to walk its connections. Grey dots: the cortex surface; pink: deep structures; blue lines: measured connections (brighter = stronger); dashed gold: known pathways with no measured strength.

What is measured, what is ours

  • Measured: the brain's shape and where each region lies (real surface), the strength of the connections between regions (diffusion MRI tractography, 207 adults), which regions each study reported for an emotion.
  • Ours: the rule by which impulses travel (more often along stronger connections), their speed, the colours, and the mapping of each study's areas onto these 82 regions (stated per emotion).
  • A region-level map, not neurons: about 86 billion neurons and no complete human neuron-level connectome exist. The finest human map here has 82 regions; the fly's page shows a real neuron-level circuit.
  • No region belongs to one emotion: studies report shared regions, and the page counts them.
