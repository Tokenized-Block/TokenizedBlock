How a round works

  • A round is committed before its own randomness exists. The contract refuses a round whose start or end is in the past, so nobody — including me — can know which block will be photographed.
  • When it ends, one past block inside it is drawn from the randomness the Ethereum beacon chain wrote into that end block. It is already mined when the result is known, so buying after the draw is useless.
  • Balances are read at that exact block, not today. The market pool is left out — at launch it holds 99.9% of a block, and nobody can withdraw it.
  • Claims open 6 hours after the root is posted, so anyone can recompute it first.
  • Nobody can take the pot out. There is no withdraw function in the contract. What is not claimed rolls into a later round, and anyone can trigger that.
