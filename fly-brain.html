Kenyon cells pluck notes, outputs hum high (approach) or low (avoid), reward rings, punishment drums. Same moves, same rhythm.

Its thoughts · before, now, next

Each decision is a set of synapses firing: the Kenyon cells the smell woke up, through the synapses it learned, into approach or avoid outputs. Tap a synapse to light it on the map and walk from there.
“Next” is computed, not guessed: the fly is deterministic, so a shadow copy of it, loaded with its exact state, is run ahead once per possible move (Laplace's demon, at the scale of this model). It forecasts the FLY's next decision, never the market: which move comes is unknown, so all three are shown and none is picked.

Let the fly trade alone · one signature

The fly gets its own small wallet, created in this browser. You sign one transaction that sends it a budget (USDC, plus a little ETH for its gas). From then on it trades that budget on its own on the stock you watch, each time it decides BUY or SELL, within your caps. Stop halts it at once; Withdraw all sends everything back to your wallet. It trades only while this page is open; reopened, it starts stopped.

How it differs from a block's brain

  • A block's brain: 128 spiking neurons whose wiring is derived from the block's own address, so every block has a brain of its own. It is fed the block's own on-chain facts (market cap, GMs, messages, new holders, whether its creator still holds) and shows how the block moves and its mood. A toy network, not a biological copy.
  • This brain: 1,119 neurons wired exactly as a real fly's, the same circuit for everyone, measured under the microscope. It is fed a stock's live market price, and it learns: dopamine reshapes its synapses, the memory forms and fades, and it leans toward or away, then meets the consequence.
  • Both: no language model, no randomness, public inputs only. Anyone can replay them and get the same result.

What this model is, and is not

  • The neurons, the synapse counts between them, and which dopamine neurons reach which output neuron are measured (electron microscopy). Threshold: 5 synapses; 300 Kenyon cells out of ~2,000, taken by ascending id.
  • The price is the stock's live on-chain pool (Aerodrome, Base), weekends included. The Chainlink reference is shown next to it, not fed to the fly.
  • The odor is what the chain proves about each transaction of the window, from its Transfers and its signer: the signer received the stock, sent it, or it moved between others. Never called "buy" or "sell". Each kind lands on its own third of the PNs (id mod 3, our choice), stronger with USD size.
  • Rises drive the reward dopamine neurons (PAM), falls the punishment ones (PPL1), as in fly studies; here they carry that teaching signal only, not the circuit's own activity. Dopamine is not spread as excitation: it weakens the Kenyon→output synapses of co-active cells, the fly's known learning rule. Its memory trace is that weakening.
  • Activity spreads in proportion to synapse counts and fades in about 3 to 4 s once a stimulus has passed; the next one waits for that. APL (inhibitory) is modelled as global inhibition that lets only the top 10% of Kenyon cells through.
  • Approach/avoid is read from output neurons sorted by their measured dopamine input. The constants, the activity rule and which PNs receive a rise or a fall (id parity) are ours: a fly does not smell prices.
  • The paper trader is virtual: no funds, no signature, ever, and not advice. Its rule: out and drawn to the smell (approach) → buy; in and repelled (avoid) → sell; otherwise hold if in, stay out if out; a window without trades changes nothing. No short. Each switch pays the pool's own fee, read on chain. It is shown next to two dumb witnesses (always in; in if the last window rose), because a P&L alone proves nothing.
  • The fly is asked before the outcome: its prediction on one window is judged on the next window's price move (under 0.05% = flat, no verdict), misses counted next to matches; then it is taught with that move, and the synapses it weakened flash white. A tendency is not a forecast and not advice. Nothing here feels anything: it is a computed state, replayable by anyone with the same data.
